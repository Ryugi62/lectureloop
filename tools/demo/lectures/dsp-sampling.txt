Last week we described signals in continuous time. But a computer can only store numbers, so we have to take samples. We measure the signal every T seconds. The sampling rate, f s, is one over T.

Here is the key idea of today. If a signal contains no frequency higher than f max, we can rebuild it perfectly from its samples, as long as we sample faster than two times f max. Two times f max is called the Nyquist rate. Please remember this sentence exactly, because this will be on the midterm. Sample faster than twice the highest frequency.

So what happens if we sample too slowly? Let me give you an example. Take a seven kilohertz tone and sample it at ten kilohertz. Half of the sampling rate is five kilohertz, so seven kilohertz is above the limit. After sampling, the tone looks exactly like a three kilohertz tone. Ten minus seven is three. That false three kilohertz tone is called an alias. And once aliasing happens, you cannot undo it. The information is gone.
