Alright, let's begin. Today's topic is Coulomb's law.

Two point charges push or pull on each other. The force is proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

In symbols, F equals k times q one times q two, divided by r squared. The constant k is about nine times ten to the ninth newton meter squared per coulomb squared.

If the charges have the same sign, they repel. If the signs are opposite, they attract.

Here is the part students always get wrong. If you double the distance, the force does not become half. It becomes one quarter. I will put an inverse square question on the midterm, I promise you that.

Force is a vector. When there are three charges, you add the forces as vectors, not as plain numbers. Draw the arrows first, then add the components.
